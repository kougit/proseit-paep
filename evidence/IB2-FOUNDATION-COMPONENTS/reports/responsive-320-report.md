## 2. Independent checks (lane D/R, round 1)

The lane ran 560 page loads: 40 pages at 320/375/834/1280/1440 px, in LTR and RTL, plus 200 % and 400 % zoom. It checked page overflow, off-screen elements, target sizes (2.5.8), clipped text and focus-ring clipping on a Tab walk. Two Medium findings came out of it: D1, where tab labels and focus were clipped at 320 px, and D2, where narrow tables broke ordinary words. Both are fixed, and D2 has a recorded residual (below). There was no page-level overflow in any configuration.

## 3. Table at 320 px (the only residual)

A per-word line-count measurement over every table cell was taken on the current build:

- At 375 px, no ordinary word breaks. Only long identifiers break, which is by design, as in render C03.
- At 320 px, with the table inside a lab card (262 px wide), words of 10 characters or fewer stay whole. The two longer words "descriptive" and "exceptionally" in the key cell break, because the status column needs 122 px and the Details column 40 px.
- Keeping whole words instead (`overflow-wrap: break-word`) makes the table 4–21 px wider than its shell, which would mean horizontal scrolling. The handoff forbids hiding the key or status column and forbids page-wide scrolling.

The behaviour is recorded as a DDR (proposed acceptable adaptation, pending the PO/SA) and as RR15.
